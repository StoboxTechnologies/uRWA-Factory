C1.2 · What lives where

Data classification and the GDPR map

The rule the whole layer is built on: the chain must hold nothing that identifies a person without the KYC Vault. Then "erasure" is real – revoke the claims, block the subject, destroy the salts and the Stripe session, and the hashes on chain point at nobody. This is the position the EDPB took in Guidelines 02/2025 on blockchain: effective anonymisation of the off-chain components, not deletion of the ledger.

Why every hash is salted

  • A country is one of ~250 values: an unsalted hash is reversed in seconds. Names and document numbers never go on chain even hashed – the EDPB treats an unkeyed hash of personal data as personal data.
  • The salt lives only in the KYC Vault. Selective disclosure works by handing one (value, salt) pair to one counterparty, who checks keccak == valueHash on chain. Same mechanism as EAS private-data attestations and the passport leaves in docs/11-passport.md.
  • The DID service SDK refuses to write a claim whose value is not a hash; a test on a fixture keeps it honest.

Roles under GDPR (counsel to confirm)

  • Controller per product (a proposal until counsel answers §8): the issuer for its instrument (Stobox Tokenized Equities Ltd for STBX; a client issuer for its token); Stobox Innovations Ltd for STBU tiers and the portal. Where the DID service is shared infrastructure, Innovations is processor for each issuer, or joint controller under Art. 26.
  • Processors: Stripe (standard DPA), database hosting, KMS.
  • Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(c) legal obligation for KYC and sanctions; 6(1)(f) legitimate interest for the on-chain hash, justified in a DPIA – the EDPB says a public chain should be used only where essential, and the argument is verification at the moment of transfer, independent readability by an auditor, no single trusted party.
  • DPIA: required – new technology, financial data, public ledger.
  • Client issuers at L1/L2 are controllers of their own KYC; we see claims only.

Mechanics that matter

  • Claim.issuer is the real writer, never a proxy, so a token can scope trust by issuer (§4).
  • Three distinguishable states – absent, expired, revoked – as the interface requires; revokeClaim by issuer or admin, block by the writer acts on every wallet of the subject.
  • Re-bind fixes handoff finding #1. bindWallet and unbindWallet emit SubjectChanged(wallet, old, new). The registry cannot know which tokens a wallet holds, so it does not block the bind; instead every token keeps accountedSubject[wallet] (one appended field in ComplianceStorage) and refuses transfers by a wallet whose accounted subject differs from the registry until anyone calls resyncSubject(wallet), which moves subjectBalance and the holder flag. MaxBalancePerHolder therefore cannot be bypassed by a re-bind.
  • Cross-chain: the same contract on Arbitrum (for STV3 through the adapter) and Base; the DID service writes both; a reconciler compares.
  • EAS is a mirror, not a second registry. On setClaim the DID service may issue an EAS attestation with the same valueHash and expiresAt for public verification on EASScan and for Base apps that speak only EAS. The registry is the source of truth.

Events (the auditor pack reads these)

  • SubjectCreated(subject, validTo) · SubjectRenewed · SubjectBlocked(subject, reasonHash)
  • WalletBound(wallet, subject) · WalletUnbound · SubjectChanged(wallet, old, new)
  • ClaimSet(subject, key, issuer, valueHash, expiresAt) · ClaimRevoked
  • IssuerTrusted(key, issuer) · IssuerUntrusted
  • Never a value, never a name. All getters are view and unprotected – the pipeline must read them – hence the invariant "no plaintext in valueHash".

Trust scoped per token (the ERC-3643 lesson)

ERC-3643 pairs a Trusted Issuers Registry with a Claim Topics Registry: each token says which topics it needs and which issuers it accepts. We get the same with less: the rules already sit on the token, so RuleLibrary gains an acceptedIssuers[] parameter (or an IssuerScopedClaim(key, issuers) rule), and the base gate can require claims[subject][urwa.identity.valid].issuer ∈ trusted(token). Portal KYC (issuer = Stobox KMS) then admits to STBU and Compass; issuer X may demand x.kyc.valid from its own key, or Stobox KYC plus us.regd.accredited from a named attestor. One registry, trust decided on the token. This closes gap T-05 in the vault.

C2.1 · From clause to code

How a right travels

A clause is extracted into a typed term with its source reference, the terms are hashed into one root that both parties sign, the root is anchored on the instrument's passport, and each enforceable or executable term is bound to a Factory rule or facet parameter. Reading goes the other way: an agent fetches the JSON, recomputes the root, checks the signatures against the chain, and only then treats the terms as the agreement.

C2.3 · The Terms model

One typed term per right, one root per instrument

Concerto-style schema serialised as JSON-LD: text, data model and logic bound through one model, as the Accord Project does, and hashed the way Ricardian contracts did in 2000 – the signed document's hash is the identifier every transaction carries. Documents are registered ERC-1643-style (name, uri, hash). The root lands on the passport, not on a new facet: the passport already owns snapshot roots, attestations and termsHash, and the question "who may anchor" is settled there.

C2.4 · Read by agents, executed within mandates

What "AI can enforce" means, and the clauses that make it binding

Reading

  • The MCP tool makes three calls: passport.termsRoot(instrumentId) from the chain; GET record/<instrumentId>/terms.json from the Intelligence record; locally recompute both roots and check the two EIP-712 signatures. Only a matching JSON counts as the agreement.
  • "Can subject X sell Y tokens today" is answered with the same code the chain runs: canTransfer and whyBlocked, plus lockupsOf. Not a PDF parse – typed terms with a back-reference to the clause.

Executing

  • AgentAuthority mandates gain scope = termId: an agent may execute dividend.pay up to N per epoch, and cannot execute a term that is not in the signed root nor exceed the amount. AgentActed + TermExecuted are the proof.
  • Today consume is called by nobody in src/; the mandate becomes real only when the payout, consent and Board contracts call it.

Legal meaning (UK Law Commission, 2021; counsel to confirm)

  • Existing contract law accommodates smart legal contracts without new statute. Code can be a term, or the way a natural-language obligation is performed; where it merely automates a term, the code is performance, not a term.
  • Courts read code through a "reasonable coder". The Commission recommends the parties themselves say which layer prevails on conflict and allocate the risk of bugs and oracles.
  • Wyoming W.S. 17-16-625 lets a corporation issue share certificates as certificate tokens on a ledger – the route by which STBX could one day rise from "economic interest through STEL" to a tokenised Class C certificate, where "right in the SPA = right in the token" becomes literal.

C3.5 · STBX

STBX is an economic participation in Class C of Stobox Technologies (WY) through STEL (Doc 3.6 §1.2), and STEL is wholly owned by a US company – almost certainly not a foreign private issuer, so the underlying is equity of a non-reporting US issuer: Reg S category 3, and Rule 905 keeps what is bought restricted after the DCP.

Three facts that change the answer

C4.5 · Agents and mandates

Who the agent is, what it may do, how it is stopped

Identity of an agent

  • A wallet bound to the principal's DID subject (access) plus attestations about the operator (trust): agent.operator – legal entity, jurisdiction, registration, uDID; agent.kya – the five Stobox Attest checks (entity exists, key control, responsible person, sanctions and adverse media, endpoint liveness). Never a score of the agent's work.
  • Spending and transfer bounds are not claims – they are the mandate: maxPerAction, maxPerEpoch, tokens[], counterparties[].
  • ERC-8004: Identity registry yes (discovery); Reputation optional and only with an Attest or payment proof behind it; Validation is where Stobox writes the KYA result. Reason: 3–15% of registered agents have a live endpoint and reputation is 259× cheaper to buy than the median payment it protects (vault note on the agentic economy).

Guardrails

  • Every action is a signed intent (EIP-712, as the DvP Instruction and the STBU voucher pattern), checked twice: AgentAuthority.check / consume (mandate) and the token's compliance pipeline (eligibility).
  • A human confirms above a threshold – the same pattern as the oracle's deviation guard and the Copilot ladder Observing → Proposing → Drafting → Acting-leashed: levels 1–3 read and prepare unsent intents; level 4 consumes within caps; above caps, the Safe.
  • Logs: AgentActed(mandateId, agent, principal, selector, amount), MandateGranted / Revoked, Settled, TransferRefused – an agent's action is never indistinguishable from a human's.

3b · Roles that move

Ruled 25.09.2026: the technical role must be transferable – to a second Safe, to a partner, to a TA – without a redeploy. Design:

D5 · Analysis

Where Orbit sets the category, where it follows, what it missed

Genuinely different – verified against the market

  • The record with tiered provenance. None of Securitize, Tokeny, Fireblocks, Chainlink, Kinexys or Canton carries tier_held, confidence, as_of, valid_until, document_id + locator and an evidenceHash on every fact. Securitize's MCP answers what exists on chain; Orbit answers who said it, when, on which document.
  • Multi-party signed oracle channels. ISSUER + RELAY + ATTESTOR / REGULATED with a threshold per instrument – a chain of responsibility a regulator can read, closer to what MAS GFIF asks than oracle-as-a-service.
  • Terms as code. A corporate action executes only if its ref is a term in the signed root. Neither DS Protocol nor T-REX links document to executable term.
  • Mandates by construction unable to mint, freeze, seize, cut or pause. Nobody in the set has it – and today it is a promise (G11).
  • An open-source factory the client deploys, with no Stobox role left on the token. DS and T-REX are open source too, but hosted; ours is designed for "we control the keys" buyers.
  • Deterministic settlement: canTransfer never reverts, whyBlocked names the rule, both legs in one transaction – the ICMA DvP target model.

Behind – said plainly

  • Custody: no integrated qualified custodian; Securitize lists BNY, Anchorage, Copper, Fireblocks.
  • Cross-chain: dropped by design ("one register"); defensible for a regulated buyer, but a "No" in every RFP row where BUIDL runs on nine chains.
  • Track record: STBX on Arbitrum, 107 holders, no audit, register rebuilt by hand; against Securitize's $3.4B AUM and a public listing.
  • Licensed venue: none; the Board is a BVI bulletin board with a perimeter question open.
  • Assurance: no SOC 1, no SOC 2, no external audit, no insurance.
  • RFP score: of 72 questions a regulated buyer sends, about 12 pass today, about 30 more after Phase 3, about 18 in Phases 4–6, and 12 never – 5 by canon (no full white-label, no venue, no percentage of a raise, no bps, no professional services for self-serve), 7 simply not done.

What "done" means, at four levels

Nothing moves to the next phase until every line of the level below is green. Acceptance is per module, delivery is per release, audit is per mainnet deployment, enterprise-grade is per year of operation.

Acceptance · per module

  • forge test green, forge fmt --check clean, branch coverage per area at the targets of doc 23 (pipeline and rules 100%, ledger 95%, treasury 90%).
  • Every fix has a test that fails when the fix is reverted (repo rule, doc 34).
  • ERC-7943 conformance kit passes on the token with the facet installed.
  • Invariants hold under fuzzing: Σ balances = Σ subjectBalance = totalSupply; frozen ≤ balance; no forced op changes a cap.
  • Identity: a Reg S preset refuses a US subject and admits a non-US subject with three linked wallets counted as one holder.
  • Oracle: a record past maxAge reads stale; a > 15% step is not readable until confirmed; sequence never repeats.
  • Emergency: forced transfer works while globally paused, refuses a recipient that is neither trusted nor active or is address-paused, moves lockups, emits Transfer then ForcedTransfer then ForcedOperation.
  • python3 verify.py and --self-test green; docs 07 and 14 updated in the same commit.

  • Deploy script in script/ runs on Base Sepolia from CI with no manual step; addresses written to docs/deployments/.
