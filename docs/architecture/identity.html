Stobox · internal architecture · STV4 layer 1 · v0.3 · 25 Sep 2026

STV4 Identity

One registry of Stobox DIDs on Base, keyed by person rather than by wallet, feeding every Compass token, the Board and STBU tiers through two read surfaces. KYC stays with Stripe and the DID service; the chain carries salted hashes, validity and claims. The gap: today's Stobox DID cannot answer a rule's question by subject, so every preset refuses every transfer on it.

1 · The identity system

From a passport scan to a transfer check

Left to right: what a person gives, where it is kept, what reaches the chain, who reads it. Nothing right of the DID service holds a name, a document or a country in clear – only hashes with a salt that lives in the KYC Vault. The blue path is the one a transfer takes: isActive and hasValidClaim read by the token's compliance pipeline.

2 · What lives where

Data classification and the GDPR map

The rule the whole layer is built on: the chain must hold nothing that identifies a person without the KYC Vault. Then "erasure" is real – revoke the claims, block the subject, destroy the salts and the Stripe session, and the hashes on chain point at nobody. This is the position the EDPB took in Guidelines 02/2025 on blockchain: effective anonymisation of the off-chain components, not deletion of the ledger.

Why every hash is salted

  • A country is one of ~250 values: an unsalted hash is reversed in seconds. Names and document numbers never go on chain even hashed – the EDPB treats an unkeyed hash of personal data as personal data.
  • The salt lives only in the KYC Vault. Selective disclosure works by handing one (value, salt) pair to one counterparty, who checks keccak == valueHash on chain. Same mechanism as EAS private-data attestations and the passport leaves in docs/11-passport.md.
  • The DID service SDK refuses to write a claim whose value is not a hash; a test on a fixture keeps it honest.

Roles under GDPR (counsel to confirm)

  • Controller per product (a proposal until counsel answers §8): the issuer for its instrument (Stobox Tokenized Equities Ltd for STBX; a client issuer for its token); Stobox Innovations Ltd for STBU tiers and the portal. Where the DID service is shared infrastructure, Innovations is processor for each issuer, or joint controller under Art. 26.
  • Processors: Stripe (standard DPA), database hosting, KMS.
  • Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(c) legal obligation for KYC and sanctions; 6(1)(f) legitimate interest for the on-chain hash, justified in a DPIA – the EDPB says a public chain should be used only where essential, and the argument is verification at the moment of transfer, independent readability by an auditor, no single trusted party.
  • DPIA: required – new technology, financial data, public ledger.
  • Client issuers at L1/L2 are controllers of their own KYC; we see claims only.
3 · The one registry

StoboxIdentityRegistry on Base

One contract, subject-keyed inside, two read surfaces outside. Surface A is IIdentityRegistry unchanged, so the Factory's pipeline and rules need no edit. Surface B is the simple view STBU tiers and the portal want. The uDID string itself is not stored: subject = keccak256(uDID) is what the adapter already computes, so a person keeps the same subject on Arbitrum and on Base.

// storage
mapping(address => bytes32)                     subjectOf;          // wallet → keccak256(uDID)

mapping(bytes32 => mapping(address => bool))    trustedIssuerForKey; // key → issuer → allowed

Mechanics that matter

  • Claim.issuer is the real writer, never a proxy, so a token can scope trust by issuer (§4).
  • Three distinguishable states – absent, expired, revoked – as the interface requires; revokeClaim by issuer or admin, block by the writer acts on every wallet of the subject.
  • Re-bind fixes handoff finding #1. bindWallet and unbindWallet emit SubjectChanged(wallet, old, new). The registry cannot know which tokens a wallet holds, so it does not block the bind; instead every token keeps accountedSubject[wallet] (one appended field in ComplianceStorage) and refuses transfers by a wallet whose accounted subject differs from the registry until anyone calls resyncSubject(wallet), which moves subjectBalance and the holder flag. MaxBalancePerHolder therefore cannot be bypassed by a re-bind.
  • Cross-chain: the same contract on Arbitrum (for STV3 through the adapter) and Base; the DID service writes both; a reconciler compares.
  • EAS is a mirror, not a second registry. On setClaim the DID service may issue an EAS attestation with the same valueHash and expiresAt for public verification on EASScan and for Base apps that speak only EAS. The registry is the source of truth.

Events (the auditor pack reads these)

  • SubjectCreated(subject, validTo) · SubjectRenewed · SubjectBlocked(subject, reasonHash)
  • WalletBound(wallet, subject) · WalletUnbound · SubjectChanged(wallet, old, new)
  • ClaimSet(subject, key, issuer, valueHash, expiresAt) · ClaimRevoked
  • IssuerTrusted(key, issuer) · IssuerUntrusted
  • Never a value, never a name. All getters are view and unprotected – the pipeline must read them – hence the invariant "no plaintext in valueHash".
4 · Pluggable both ways

A client brings its own KYC; others read ours

Gene's requirement: an enterprise client says "we have our own KYC data, we will not share it, connect us to DID and we run it ourselves". Three integration levels, from cheapest to most independent, and the reverse direction with one safeguard: trust scoped per token, so that portal KYC does not admit a wallet to every issuer's instrument.

Trust scoped per token (the ERC-3643 lesson)

ERC-3643 pairs a Trusted Issuers Registry with a Claim Topics Registry: each token says which topics it needs and which issuers it accepts. We get the same with less: the rules already sit on the token, so RuleLibrary gains an acceptedIssuers[] parameter (or an IssuerScopedClaim(key, issuers) rule), and the base gate can require claims[subject][urwa.identity.valid].issuer ∈ trusted(token). Portal KYC (issuer = Stobox KMS) then admits to STBU and Compass; issuer X may demand x.kyc.valid from its own key, or Stobox KYC plus us.regd.accredited from a named attestor. One registry, trust decided on the token. This closes gap T-05 in the vault.

Others reading Stobox DID

  • Publish the interface (MIT, like the rest of the Factory), the addresses, the event schema and the EAS mirror.
  • Publish a Stobox Identity Trust Framework: who issues, what each key means, how revocation works, SLAs, what stands behind urwa.identity.valid (Stripe + sanctions + residence), and what we do not assert.
  • A reader outside Stobox gets exactly what a Compass token gets: hashes, validity, issuer. Disclosure of a value is always a bilateral, off-chain act by the person or by us under contract.

Not to be promised anywhere. "Stobox never sees the data" (we are the KYC controller); "full anonymity" (a regulated security needs a named register at the transfer agent or the BVI registrar); "deleted from the chain"; "ZK-KYC in production" before a circuit audit. Institutions in 2026 ask who the controller is, where PII sits, what the DPA with the provider says, how revoke and erasure run, and whether they can bring their own KYC. Securitize and Tokeny both keep KYC off chain and whitelist on chain; ZK is on nobody's critical path.

8 · Decisions

For Gene, and for counsel

Gene

  1. Drop the uDID string on chain – bytes32 only; 4XR and STBU move to the new getter. Yes or no?
  2. Controller of KYC: Innovations as operator, or the issuer per product? Decides who signs the DPIA and answers erasure requests.
  3. Trust scoping – ruled 25.09 by principle ("everything as securities law requires"): the base gate isActive stays universal, so one KYC admits a person to Stobox as such; every securities preset requires issuer-scoped claims in its rules, so admission to an instrument is decided by the issuers that instrument trusts. Portal KYC therefore opens STBU tiers and the Board's read side, never a security by itself.
  4. Re-bind with a balance – settled by the technical review: the token records accountedSubject per wallet and refuses any transfer by a wallet whose accounted subject differs from the registry until resyncSubject runs; the registry itself does not block the bind.
  5. EAS mirror from day one or after launch?
  6. ZK pilot budget in 2027, or wait for a regulated issuer?

Max (counsel)

  1. Controller / processor allocation across Innovations (BVI), STE Ltd (BVI) and Technologies (WY); joint control?
  2. KYC retention under BVI AMLR versus the EU five years; may the Stripe session be deleted earlier while the result is kept?
  3. Is "effective anonymisation" by salt destruction a sufficient Art. 17 answer with an immutable hash, per EDPB v2?
  4. Transfer basis for PII from BVI to Stripe (US) and to an EU database.
  5. Liability of an L1 issuer to the issuer of the token for a false claim; our recourse.
  6. Does portal KYC (Stripe) equal investor KYC for Reg S and a BVI private placement, and who attests us.regd.accredited?
